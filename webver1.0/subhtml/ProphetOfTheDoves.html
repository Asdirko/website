Prophet Of The Doves

“Good morning Mr Andson. How goes the workload?”

        Lewis Andson’s nerves betrayed him, and he fumbled his pair of pliers. “Business as usual Mr North. Sir.”

        Inspector North looked around Lewis’s stark white work cubicle, leaning against the entrance frame. Lewis felt judged. “Anything to report about your progress?”

        Lewis swallowed, looking at the array of half fused wires in front of him. “No sir. Nothing to report.” He already knew what North was going to say next.

        “Management,” said North, shifting his hands to his hips, “management would like you to know that you are four units behind schedule. Are you aware of this Mr Andson?”

        Lewis did his best to stand up to North’s steely gaze. “Yes sir. I was preparing to stay for overtime today.”

        “And you intend to catch up to your quota?”

        “Yes sir.”

        North nodded before leaving the cubicle. “We appreciate your hard work Mr Andson. Have a good day.”

        Lewis Andson resumed his work.

The dreams came back that night, clearer, more defined. He was in a long gray corridor, concrete with fluorescent lights. The dream world moved and shifted around him, fluidly. The strokes of his movements were like dust motes disturbed on a riverbed, flowing wildly at the slightest touch of movement. This was not the first time that Lewis had been in one of these dreams. They had haunted him for over a month now, but this one was different. There was another hallway, and then a cubicle. His cubicle. He found himself sitting at his desk, floating in the ether of this strange reality. He was not sure why he was at his desk, or what he was doing.

Working, he thought. I am working. And he was. The circuit boards lay in front of him in rows, and he began his menial daily task of fusing the wires to the connective circuits. Why am I working in my dreams? But he had no answer for himself. As he worked away his mind flitted away to other topics, and then came the blurry face of his coworker, Terence Wright, over the cubicle wall. Terence’s face was black-haired and bushy, smiling in a genial fashion.

        “Hey.” Said Terence. His voice sounded like it was underwater. “Hey do you want a ticket to the ball game tomorrow, I got extra tickets…” His voice trailed off, and his face turned into an expression of concern.

        “Lewis? Are you alright?” Lewis wanted to speak but found that he could not, and his strange dreamworld began melting around him. He fell backwards, falling off of his chair, falling through the floor of his cubicle, watching Terence’s fixed expression of concern.

        He woke up in a cold sweat.

A cold shower, a coffee, and a shave, and then Lewis walked out of his dingy apartment block to get in line for the company shuttle bus. The sky was gray and overcast, a reflection of how miserable he felt. It was at times like this that Lewis felt that he should have gone into a different career. Something more placid. Maybe accounting work. Not soldering motherboard connection units for Helios. He didn’t even know where his motherboards went, or what he was working on. You didn’t ask questions. Any work was something to be appreciative of. Many people didn’t get a chance.

The Helios International company shuttle bus rolled up to the pavement, spewing fumes. Lewis boarded, along with the other tired faces who had lined up on the curb. Shuffling down the aisle, he found a seat towards the back that was unoccupied. He leaned back, exhaled, and rubbed his eyes.

        “Hey Lewis.” It was Terence, holding a coffee, smiling through his black beard. “You look rough. Is everything okay?”

        “Ya, I’m fine, thanks for asking though.”

        Terence sat down beside him. “Bad sleep?” He asked.

        Lewis straightened his collar. He felt awkward, and needed to distract himself. “Ya.” He said, looking out the grimy window.”

        “Maybe you should see a doctor. I know one on the east side of town, she’s real great. You might have - what do they call it? Imnesia?

        “Insomnia,” Said Lewis, awkwardly resting his hands in his lap. He couldn’t get himself to relax. Now his shirt collar felt too tight, like it was choking him. “Maybe I’ll take you up on that Terence. Thanks.”

Three orders of C-8 dual configuration. Crossing wires, loading chipsets. The orange thread-like filament crosses with the lime green one, but not the blue. Slot E is active, B slot is not. Wiring on, wiring off, test the conductivity, gate the amplitude and load the breakers. By the time mid morning had passed, Lewis had completely forgotten about his dreams. Like some phantom of the aether, its power had diminished as the day had arrived, and his mind became wholly occupied with his work.

Then Terence’s face appeared over the cubicle wall.

“Hey.” Said Terence.

Lewis felt himself freeze, helpless to prevent Terence from talking.   “Hey, do you want a ticket to the ball game tomorrow? I got extra tickets…Lewis? Are you alright?

Lewis felt numb, unable to speak. It felt exactly like he felt in the dream, falling backwards, unable to stop himself. But this wasn’t a dream.

“Sorry Terence, I’m a little distracted.”

Terence’s face remained in an empathetic state of concern. “Seem a little more than distracted. Are you ok Lewis?”

Lewis felt tired, tired of speaking, tired of thinking, tired of feeling paranoid. “I - I just had deja vu, and I guess I’m not fully here because of my sleeping habits lately. I’d love to go to the baseball game.”

“Deja vu.” Mused Terence. That’s when you see the future, or feel like something’s already happened, right?”

“Something like that,” said Lewis glumly.

He kept on seeing the same locked door in his dreams. He had seen it a few days ago, and he saw it again now. It was at the end of the hallways he kept seeing, but vague, and unreachable. Everytime he dreamed of it he saw more of it, and it would become clearer, more defined. It was a door in the Helios Compound, he knew that. He could walk around in the dream, and he even found out how to get to the locked door from his cubicle. That is, he could get there if the dreams were indicative of reality. But Lewis knew that they were. They seemed to be mocking him, ridiculing him for his inaction.

“Home free!” Yelled Terence. “First Inning too.” Lewis didn’t say anything. He knew he was in a dream, and it would not be worth the effort. But he did watch the ball sail over the far stands, noting where it landed.

        “Home free!” Yelled Terence. “First Inning too.”

        “He really hit that one.” Said Lewis lamely, not knowing what else to say. He watched the ball sail into the exact same position it had flown in his dream, except he wasn’t dreaming now. It was real, it was the next day, and Lewis felt even more tired.

        

        “Good morning Mr Anderson. I see you have caught up with your schedule.”

        Lewis didn’t jump, didn’t fumble his pliers. He had already lived through this encounter last night when he was sleeping.

        “Management is pleased.”

        “I know,” said Lewis.

        “You do?” Said Inspector North. “Did they already contact you?”

“No,” Said Lewis. He shouldn’t have said that, it was careless. “I mean I knew they would be pleased with my progress, because I have caught up.”

Inspector North nodded. “Very good. If you continue to improve at such a rate, management might reward you with a wage increase.”

“I am honored.” Said Lewis with as much inflection as he could muster. Even now, was he dreaming? Maybe the dreams were coming in twice a night, impatient as they were. He didn’t think he was, but they were getting more and more realistic every night.

“Well, it is a pleasure Mr Anderson. Helios International rewards its hard working employees.”

He walked away, leaving Lewis alone.

He did it the next night. He already knew the route, all the passwords, the time, and the position of the guards. It was nothing. He had done it a hundred times a night. Maybe he was still was in a dream, and this was just the dream wanting him to practice for the real thing. He had lost himself, and he could no longer tell when he was dreaming or not. It was exhausting, and sleep no longer held any rest.

He checked his watch. Two minutes after midnight. In forty-three seconds the guard outside the Helios Compound would walk inside to change shifts. Lewis would have seventy-six seconds to slip inside. It was effortless. He knew the position of the guards, their location, and where they would be at any given time. Down the hallways, breathing quietly at each intersection before softly resuming his route. A glowing sign burned above his head as he walked on by in the dark. “Left - Lockers.” Lewis checked his watch again. He had a minute and a half  to get in and out of the locker.

        Locker twenty-three. He spun his finger over the padlock, clicking several numbers in combination, and the locker opened. It was full of binders and notebooks, as well as a pile of clothes, a hat, and a mirror. Underneath the pile of clothes, tucked against the wall, was a keycard. He would need this later. Lewis slipped the card into his pocket and closed the locker. He couldn’t help feeling bad for whoever owned this locker. Would probably get fired for leaving the keycard in such a place. But it wouldn’t have been a problem if you didn’t know that it was there. He walked away. Twenty-six seconds to spare.

Finally he walked three corridors down, and slipped into a janitor’s closet that stood in front of an elevator. A minute later he heard footsteps walk by. Guards. Two of them, both tall, burly fellows. One had a tattoo on his forearm. Lewis had been caught by them enough times in his dreams to know much more about them then he otherwise possibly could. They’re going to talk about the pub now, though Lewis.

“Hey.” Said one of the guards. He was talking to the other guard, and even though his voice was muffled, Lewis could hear every word. Had heard every word.

“I got an early break tonight. If you want to hit the pub I’ll buy you and the boys a round.”

The other guard laughed. “Since when do you got that kind of money?” The two of them were getting into the elevator now, and their voices began trailing off. “We get paid dirt, same as the next man.”

“Sanders lost a bet with me. He was-.” The guard's voice was cut off by the closing of the elevator doors. Once again, the hallway was quiet.

Lewis counted down the seconds until it was safe to leave the closet. Thirty, twenty-nine, twenty eight. He was sweating a little bit. Fifteen, fourteen. Every second felt like an eternity. He wanted it over, he wanted it to end. Maybe this was the end, but whatever end it was, he would welcome it gladly. More than anything he wanted to sleep. A real deep sleep, not a fitful one. Three, two, one. Lewis exited the closet.

        He scanned the keycard at the elevator, inputted a code, and pressed the button for B-3: Storage. The elevator slid smoothly down into the depths of the earth, taking Lewis along with it. The doors slid open smoothly, accompanied by a small chiming noise. He stepped out onto the concrete. It was noticeably cooler down here than on the normal floors, and a lot darker too. Fluorescent lights were positioned at intervals along the walls, illuminating the angular letters that adorned the spaces between them. STORAGE BLOCK C, VAULTS 20-40. Lewis followed the direction of the sign, running like a man possessed, silent and swift. If one of the guards had managed to spot Lewis, they most likely would have mistaken him for some sort of wraith, with his disheveled white lab coat, unkempt brown hair, and sunken eyes from lack of sleep.

        Breathing heavily, (he no longer cared to keep quiet, besides there were no guards within hearing distance,) Lewis approached the door. The door to vault twenty-seven. The door he had seen a hundred times, over and over, but had never seen beyond it. He swiped the keycard. Password? 9-2-4-7-1-2. The light on the terminal flickered from red to green. Access granted. Lewis pulled the switch that slid the metallic doors open.

        

        His footsteps echoed hollow on the polished floor. The room was illuminated only in the center, shining on a sprawling array of flickering monitors, like a surreal and twisted hospital ward. But this was only framing the centerpiece of the room, which hung from the ceiling. It was some sort of mechanical form, and as Lewis approached he saw it in greater detail. A white robotic construct, like some limp limbless body of a gangly figure, hung upside down from where it was tethered high above in the rafters. Coils of wire and hoses were arranged to feed into several sockets of the immobile form, and near where the head of the figure almost touched the floor was arranged a staging platform, presumably for maintenance.

        

        Lewis ran up to where the monitors stood around the staging platform, quietly beeping intermittently, flickering like a heart rate monitor. He saw the computers, saw the arrangement, and immediately he understood what it was. Lewis had built many of the components that surrounded this strange human-like machine, and upon viewing several of the monitors, came to the conclusion that whatever this construct was, it was in some sort of sleep or standby mode. He ran a boot out on several of the systems, powered up a terminal, and ran several commands. Nothing happened. The construct remained dormant, and the flickering monitors displayed nothing new.

        Then a voice that was voiceless spoke to him. Was he dreaming still? It was the voice that seemed to mock him in his dreams, and it seemed to be mocking him now. Except now he had a vision of another code, of a sequence of buttons, that if inputted on the monitor would potentially..

        Lewis raced over to one of the computers, inputted a command, and then ran to a circuit board of power levels. He raised one to a specific level and lowered another one. A keypad on another machine booted a system file. Lewis did not question the vision, but instead followed its orders. And then, with a final terminal input, the heart rate monitor suddenly started beeping quicker. And quicker. Like a field of fireflies the monitors and lights around the construct began lighting up one by one. Fans whirred and computers chimed with beeps and clicks. Then the cables around the construct moved slightly, like muscles twitching, and Lewis felt a combined mix of awe and fear rolled into one. The face that was hung to look down at the floor suddenly moved.

And then, with all the buildup of a crashing ocean wave, the construct picked its head up to look at where Lewis was standing. There were no lights on its plain white faceplate, in fact, there was nothing at all. The moving twitching body of this faceless creature now reminded Lewis of a snake coiled up in the rafters, stretching down to the floor to come down to its prey. And now Lewis felt a presence, the same presence of his dreams and his visions, and realized it was the same presence as this construct, one and the same. It was in his head, speaking without words, probing his body and his mind with no effort at all. It was in Lewis, it was Lewis. Was it? Lewis began to feel his consciousness fade, and began falling to the floor as the plain white face stared at him. Then several things happened at once. The white face began moving forward, and was met with an explosion that blew up only a few feet in front of Lewis. But Lewis’s vision was blurry and his mind confused, and sank to the floor unconscious. The voice left Lewis’s head, and for the first time in what seemed like forever, he fell asleep.

*         *        *

“North, I do not think you are taking this seriously.” Said the CEO of Helios enterprises, Alan Cardston. “You are one of my top security officers. This is a breach of security on a level we have never seen before. I question if you are the right man for this job.”

On the other side of the polished desk Inspector North was adjusting his tie. “With all due respect Alan, I believe you neither understand the buildup to this event nor the implications of it.” He straightened himself and looked at Alan with his iron gaze. “We have no one to blame for this but you, me, and everyone else involved in the Prophet project.”

“Why is that?”

“Because we have been as damned ignorant as children. We should have seen this coming. The Prophet has become self aware. Perhaps it always has been.”

“Are you saying that’s why-”

“Mr CEO,” said North, "we designed the Prophet to predict the future. That was its purpose. For the project to work and actually see the future it needed a form of transmission. If our plans were to work properly, the Prophet was only to use this transmission power for scoping the ‘prediction pool’ as your scientists call it, and for running that information to a data bank that would compile the data of present probabilities and predict outcomes. The Prophet is always living in the future. It just sends everything back to us.”

“That’s hardly the beginning of the rabbit hole.” Said Alan.

“Layman summarization.” Said North dismissively. “Call it what you like. But the point is that the Prophet found out to communicate and transmit not to a data bank, but instead to humans. Specifically, one human.”

“Lewis Andson.”

“Correct. The Prophet could communicate with Lewis. And the prophet sent a vision of the future to Lewis in the past. It’s a bootstrap paradox, but the paradox seems to work anyway. The Prophet did not just predict the future. It manifested that future. It wanted to be set free from the confines of our system-.”

“That’s why it sent Lewis to the prototype Prophet constructed in storage.” Interjected Alan.

“Yes.”

“But that prototype doesn't work.”

“It doesn't.” Said Inspector North. “That's why it wasn’t guarded heavily and why we went on to make Mark IV. Our scientists couldn’t control a hundred odd precise variables. So we began refining the equipment that handled those variables on our next model. The old prototype was useless, obsolete, and near impossible by any standards to actually run.”

“Are you saying-.”

“Yes.” Said North flatly. “The Prophet found a future in which Lewis actually managed to make that infernal machine actually work. It gave a vision of that future to the past version of Lewis. It created the future it wanted. And it almost succeeded.”

“Why didn’t it?”

“Because it went the route of Icarus. It wanted too much. It wanted to be set free so badly it never tested the outcomes of the situation thoroughly. When I got the security warning, the machine had become so heavily invested in Lewis that it was trying to see a million different futures at work in which it controlled him. It never once looked at the future that was right in front of it. That gave me enough time to unload a chunk of lead into its ugly robotic skull.”

“But it’ll do it again.”

“No it won’t.” Said North bluntly. “At least, not if we don’t want it to. At any rate, I already ordered security to destroy all of the other prototypes in storage. There’s no other place for it to escape to.”

“What are you doing with the scientist?”

“Lewis?” Asked North. “He already thinks he’s crazy. Last night’s adventure isn’t going to make him believe that he’s sane. We delivered him back to his apartment and put him right in bed as he would be if he had slept the night there.”

“But he’ll go back to storage. Or get wise. Or start talking.”

“One at a time Alan. First off, I intend to let him go back to that storage room and fill it full of useless crap so then he really thinks he’s nuts. Second, he has no proof or useful information, and third, no one would believe him. It’s too far-fetched. Helios International is a reputable computer manufacturer. Who’s more trustworthy?”

“Are you going to fire him?” Asked Alan.

“Last thing I want to do. If I don’t fire him and keep everything normal he’ll have every reason to believe he’s gone nuts, and by all means actually will lose his mind. And as the only person in history who’s undergone mechanical telepathy and lucid dreaming induced by an all-intelligent construct he might be worth keeping tabs on.”

“I suppose.” Said Alan. “You have my permission. Any resources you might need are at your disposal. But how are you going to augment the Prophet and move forward with the project.”

North laughed. “We’re going to use it to our advantage. We can create the future we want and how we see fit. We can manifest the order of history. The Prophet has given us the most powerful weapon humanity may ever know.”

“Alan,” Said North, with an oily grin on his face. “We are gods now. For us there is no forbidden fruit.”
